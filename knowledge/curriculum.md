# Tutor Lab curriculum design

Lessons teach before they assess. Each unit has: a learning goal, why it matters, visual concept, truth table or timing diagram, guided circuit, prediction prompt, editable practice, short explanation of the scan result, then a transfer task. Keep optional deeper notes for vendor-specific differences.

## Proposed progression

1. Read rails, rung and tags; distinguish field input from program output.
2. NO and NC contacts, polarity and truth tables.
3. Series AND; parallel OR; compound branches.
4. OTE coil and why a driven output is overwritten; scan order.
5. Multiple rungs and controlled writes.
6. Set/reset coils, seal-in circuits, safe stop paths and conflicting writers.
7. Rising/falling edges, one-shots and event counting.
8. TON and elapsed time; timer status contacts.
9. TOF, TP, retentive timing and reset behavior.
10. CTU/CTD, presets, accumulated counts, reset; edge-triggered increments.
11. Comparisons and number tags; arithmetic and MOVE.
12. Integrated sequence challenges and troubleshooting.

Learning objectives are our curriculum decisions, not copied lesson text. Extend with diagnostics and explanation before raising circuit complexity.
